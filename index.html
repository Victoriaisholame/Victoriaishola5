Her Life Story

Late Mrs. Victoria Olaitan Ishola (affectionately known as Iya Wisdom) was born in Kumasi, Gold Coast (now Ghana), to the family of Late Pa James Olahan Solademi and Late Mama Comfort Ajibade Solademi of Ilé Alaso Isaale Laka, Ogbomoso, Oyo State. Both of her parents were enterprising traders in Kumasi, where she was raised in a home steeped in hard work, discipline, and godly values.

Mama began her education at Edum Presbyterian Primary School, Kumasi, and later transferred to First Baptist Church Primary School upon its establishment. In 1969, following the Ghanaian repatriation policy, she relocated with her parents to Ogbomoso. Demonstrating early diligence and artistic skill, she acquired expertise in tailoring, embroidery, and knitting, which became vital tools of enterprise throughout her lifetime. She also resided in Ibadan with her uncle, working diligently while cultivating her passion for commerce.

Mrs. Victoria Olaitan Ishola surrendered her life to Jesus Christ, was baptised by immersion, and remained an unshakable pillar in God’s vineyard. At Ebenezer Baptist Church, Kakuri, Kaduna, she served as Choir Member, Girls' Auxiliary (G.A.) Leader, WMU Vice Chairperson, and WMU President (1997–2000). She also led the Ayaba Esteri Group and Imole Tan Group. Following her relocation to Abuja, she served as WMU President of First Baptist Church, Gwagwalada (2007–2009), Treasurer of the Amazing Grace Baptist Association (2010–2013), and President of Ogbomoso Parapo Women in Kaduna (1998–2001).

Mama shared 50 golden years of marital bliss with her devoted husband, Mr. M.A. Ishola. Their marriage was anchored on unconditional love, mutual respect, and fervent prayer. Following civil unrest in Kaduna, the Holy Spirit led the family to relocate to Gwagwalada, Abuja, in 2001. A fearless prayer warrior, compassionate mentor, peacemaker, and hospitable matriarch, Mama’s life reflected the wisdom of Deborah, the selfless service of Dorcas, and the peace of Abigail. Her enduring legacy lives on through her family, church, and community.

THE LAMP THAT STILL GIVES LIGHT
An ode in loving memory of Late Mrs Victoria Olaitan Ishola
I A vessel arrived in Kumasi’s early light,
Where Gold Coast rivers sang of grace to come,
She walked through youth with faith as pure as bright,
And made the warmth of heaven in her home.
Through Ogbomoso’s soil her roots took hold,
An olive tree that bore righteous fruit;
Her hands forged treasures richer far than gold,
While selfless love remained her deepest root. II Beside her love of fifty golden years,
Matching in soul, in vesture, and in grace,
They knelt to lift their children’s joys and fears,
And turned their humble shop to holy space.
Through Kaduna’s dark storms and changing tide,
To Gwagwalada’s calm and peaceful ground,
She stood, a steadfast pillar at his side,
Where endless peace and quiet strength were found. III Her hands of skill would weave the threads of care,
With needle, cloth, and tailor’s sharp design;
Yet far more rich the tapestry of prayer
She embroidered for her lineage divine.
Each midnight hour she stood upon the height,
Iya Wisdom, whose knees fought every foe,
A lamp of truth that burned into the night
To light the paths her children were to go. IV How sweet the laughter echoing down the hall,
The divided sweet that taught us how to share,
The gentle rhythm of her daily call,
The soothing balm of her maternal care!
“Izzy Bobo,” “David Darling,” “Jafubolo” sweet,
“Iya Bẹ̀tẹ̀,” “Prep,” and songs for young ones sung—
Each tender name she spoke made love complete,
A sacred language on her mother’s tongue. V “Ìṣẹ́ kí í pa ni,” her voice would softly teach,
A well of wisdom where the thirsty drank;
She sought the broken, bridged the silent breach,
And gave to all, though lower in their rank.
Like Deborah in counsel, Dorcas in her deed,
An Abigail in peace when shadows fell,
She sowed in every heart a godly seed,
And watched the harvest of the righteous swell. VI When June’s cool evening whispered of the end,
She waited till her firstborn reached her side;
With final prayers for those she must commend,
She closed her eyes in victory, not in pride.
Though weeping lingers through the heavy night
And empty sits her chair beside her mate,
She stepped across the veil to uncreated light,
To wear the crown before the golden gate. VII Her voice is hushed, yet midnight prayers still ring,
Her hands are still, yet every stitch endures;
The seeds she sowed now bloom in endless spring,
And in her children’s hearts her reign secures.
For death can never claim what faith has sown,
Nor harvest fields that bloom in holy air;
She lives within the lives that were her own,
And guards her home through legacy and prayer. VIII So rest in peace, beloved mother, wife, and guide,
Where parting tears shall cloud the eyes no more;
Your lamp will shine beyond the swelling tide,
Your footsteps echo on the golden shore.
For love like yours outlives the mortal breath,
And character outshines the shadow, death.

> The righteous never truly leave—
They live in every life they changed.
